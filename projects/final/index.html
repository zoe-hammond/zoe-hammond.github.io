BGE draws criticism over rising rates and layoffs

Baltimore Gas and Electric opened 2026 with increasing rates and prices, along with laying off 68 employees in February, but reported record-high profits.

"It’s hard to breathe, for a long period of time, where you don’t have to have an increase in something,” said Barbara Glanzmann of Landsdowne, Maryland.

Reasons for higher rates

Maryland’s Public Service Commission approved a $53 million increase for BGE as part of a multi-year plan to increase gas and electric distribution rates during the end of last year. This plan will allow for enhanced technology for safety and reliability, along with covering increased operating costs and system modernization.

Beginning in February, BGE’s gas distribution rates have increased and will continue to increase by 72 cents for residential electric customers and almost $2 per month for residential gas customers until the end of 2027.

BGE Benefits v. Resident’s Burdans

The parent company of BGE, Exelon, reported a financial performance that "exceeded expectations,” according to its latest earnings report. The report also showed a drastic increase in BGE’s net income which skyrocketed to $578 million in 2025, from $527 million in 2024.

Although BGE is doing well according to the earnings report, “it’s been a lot of growing pains,” said Glanzmann.

Glanzmann left her home in Pennsylvania to return to her childhood home in Lansdowne to take care of her elderly parents three years ago. Since, her mother has passed away and Glanzmann still takes care of her 97 year-old father who has COPD, congestive heart failure, and early onset dementia.

“In another year or so, it’s going to be tough for us to afford it here,” Glanzmann said.

Glanzmann’s father, who is on a fixed income, asked her 10 years ago to handle his finances. Her and her father had to pull from his retirement fund in order to make ends meet. “It’s difficult on a fixed income,” Glanzmann said.

Glanzmann decided to do the budget-plan that BGE offers. She said when she began the budget within the past year, the monthly bills were $332. Within the past couple months, Glanzmann said it increased to $425 a month.

On April 22, she was given a notice that stated: “Based on a review of your account, your new budget billing amount will be $637, effective next month.”

“You do the budget, then you see that the budget is going up.”

She has decided to move her father home to Pennsylvania with her in order to be closer to her husband and only have to deal with the finances of one home. “I’m just trusting and praying this is a good move,” Glanzmann said.

What has BGE done to fix it?

The gas and electric company has made attempts to ease the rising burden on customers. In January, BGE launched an education campaign to help customers understand why their energy bills are rising.

“The purpose of this campaign is education,” said Exelon President and CEO Calvin Butler in a press release. “Marylanders deserve clear, honest information about why energy costs are rising and which parts of the system are within the state’s control. Informed conversations lead to better decisions—and families can’t afford confusion as this challenge grows.”

In the press release, the company states that the campaign is designed to support discussions among customers and policymakers about how the state can address the challenges in the months and years ahead.

“Our customers are at the center of every decision we make,” BGE President and CEO Tamla Olivier said in the press release. "This campaign is about giving Marylanders the facts — plainly, directly, and without spin — so they can advocate for an energy system that puts their needs first.”

Disadvantaging Employees

After recording record-high profits, in February BGE laid off 68 employees including 47 union workers. The company said in a press release in December that it decided to cut back, or “pause certain workstreams, which will lead to a reduction in work.”

"We do not take this decision lightly and recognize that it affects people's lives," Oliver said in a statement. "What has always made BGE special is our people, the way we show up for one another, for our customers, and for our community. This is a difficult moment, but I'm confident in our ability to come together and continue to deliver on our purpose."

Utility RELIEF Act

On April 13, Maryland lawmakers passed the Utility RELIEF Act. The act was implemented to promote local energy generation and renewable energy sources.

“That is why we are taking action where we can: holding utility companies accountable, moving faster to build new energy generation, and delivering $100 million to lower energy bills for Maryland families,” said Gov. Wes Moore.

The act is aimed to save Maryland residents and families at least $150 a year.

“In my opinion, it’s a joke,” said Bill Loveridge of Severn. “People that are getting five or six hundred dollar bills a month, that $150 a year is only about $13 a month. I don’t think it’s going to help anyone with anything. It’s politics, it’s just for show.”
